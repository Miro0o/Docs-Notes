## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Europe

The **history of Europe** is traditionally divided into four time periods: [prehistoric Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Europe "Prehistoric Europe") (prior to about 800 BC), [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") (800 BC to AD 500), the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") (AD 500–1500), and the [modern era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_era "Modern era") (since AD 1500).

The first [early European modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_modern_humans "Early European modern humans") appear in the fossil record about 48,000 years ago, during the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic") era. Settled agriculture marked the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") era, which spread slowly across Europe from southeast to the north and west. The later Neolithic period saw the introduction of early [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy "Metallurgy") and the use of copper-based tools and weapons, and the building of [megalithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith "Megalith") structures, as exemplified by [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge "Stonehenge"). During the [Indo-European migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations "Indo-European migrations"), Europe saw migrations from the east and southeast. The period known as [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") began with the emergence of the [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polis "Polis") of [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece"). Later, the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") came to dominate the entire [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin "Mediterranean Basin"). The [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period "Migration Period") of the [Germanic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_people "Germanic people") began in the late 4th century AD.

The [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire "Fall of the Western Roman Empire") in AD 476 traditionally marks the [start of the Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages "Early Middle Ages"). While the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire "Eastern Roman Empire") would continue for another 1000 years, the former lands of the Western Empire would be fragmented into the [barbarian kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_kingdoms "Barbarian kingdoms"). The [Umayyads conquered Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania "Umayyad conquest of Hispania") and established [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus"). In 800, [Pope Leo III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_III "Pope Leo III") crowned the King of the Franks, [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne"), as [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor "Holy Roman Emperor"). The [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age "Viking Age") saw a second great migration of [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen "Norsemen") peoples. The oldest university currently in continuous operation in the world was established during the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages "High Middle Ages"), which also saw two centuries of [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") to try to retake the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant") from the Muslim states that occupied it, [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism "Feudalism") reaching its height and the [Mongol invasion of 1236-1242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe "Mongol invasion of Europe"). The [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages "Late Middle Ages") were marked by a large population decline, as Europe experienced both the [Great Famine of 1315–1317](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_of_1315%E2%80%931317 "Great Famine of 1315–1317") and [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") (1346 to 1353). At the end of the Middle Ages, there was a transitional period, known as the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance").

[Early modern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_Europe "Early modern Europe"), usually dated to the end of the 15th century, began with revolutions in both [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press#Mass_production_and_spread_in_Europe "Printing press") and [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery"). [Gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder") changed how warfare was conducted and printing changed how knowledge was created, preserved and disseminated. The [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation") saw the fragmentation of religious thought and then [nearly two centuries of religious wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wars_of_religion "European wars of religion"). The [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") led to [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_of_European_colonialism_and_colonization "Analysis of European colonialism and colonization"), and the exploitation of the people and resources of colonies brought resources and wealth to Western Europe. Modern science emerged during the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution") of the 17th century. The [Age of Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Revolution "Age of Revolution") saw long-established political systems overturned. After 1800, the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") brought [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(economics\) "Capital (economics)") accumulation and rapid [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization") to Western Europe, while several countries transitioned away from [absolutist rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutism_\(European_history\) "Absolutism (European history)") to [parliamentary regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_parliamentarism#Spread_of_parliamentarism_in_Europe "History of parliamentarism"). The five great powers of the [Concert of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_of_Europe "Concert of Europe") dominated the [long nineteenth century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_nineteenth_century "Long nineteenth century"). In the 20th century, [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") led to a remaking of the map of Europe as the large empires were broken up into [nation states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state"). Lingering political issues would lead to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), during which [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") perpetrated [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"). The subsequent [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") saw Europe divided by the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain "Iron Curtain") into capitalist and communist states, led by the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") respectively, and the creation of [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") and the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact"). The West's remaining colonial empires [were dismantled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation "Decolonisation"). The last decades saw the fall of remaining dictatorships in Western Europe and a [gradual political integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration "European integration"), which led to the [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community "European Community"), later the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"). After the [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989 "Revolutions of 1989"), all European communist states [transitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_economy "Transition economy") to capitalism, and in 1991 the Soviet Union came to an end. The 21st century began with most of the former communist states [gradually joining the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_of_the_European_Union "Expansion of the European Union"). In the 2010s and 2020s, Europe has faced the [Euro area crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_area_crisis "Euro area crisis"), the [2015 European migrant crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_migrant_crisis "2015 European migrant crisis"), and the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine").
