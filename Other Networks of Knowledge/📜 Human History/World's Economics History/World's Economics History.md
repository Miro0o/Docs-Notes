## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_the_world

The **economic history of the world** encompasses the development of human [economic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_activity "Economic activity") throughout time. It has been estimated that throughout prehistory, the world average [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita "GDP per capita") was about $158 [per annum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_annum "Per annum") (inflation adjusted for 2013), and did not rise much until the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"). [Cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle") were probably the first object or physical thing specifically used in a way similar enough to the modern definition of [money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money "Money"), that is, as a [medium for exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_for_exchange "Medium for exchange").

By the 3rd millennium BC, [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") was home to almost half of the global population. The [city states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_states "City states") of [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") developed a trade and market [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy "Economy") based originally on the ancient coin, usually of silver, of the [shekel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekel "Shekel") which was a certain weight measure of [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley "Barley"), while the [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonians "Babylonians") and their city state neighbors later developed the earliest system of prices using a measure of various commercial [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(business\) "Product (business)") that was fixed in a [legal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_code "Legal code"). The early law codes from Sumer could be considered the first (written) financial law, and had many attributes still in use in the current [price system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_system "Price system") today. Temples are history's first documented creditors at interest, beginning in [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") in the third millennium. Later, in their embassy functions, they legitimized profit‑seeking trade, as well as by being a major beneficiary. According to [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus"), and most modern scholars, the [Lydians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydians "Lydians") were the first people to introduce the use of gold and silver coin around 650–600 BC.

The first economist (at least from within opinion generated by the evidence of extant writings) is considered to be [Hesiod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesiod "Hesiod"), by the fact of his having written on the fundamental subject of the scarcity of resources, in _[Works and Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_and_Days "Works and Days")_.

Eventually, the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") accounted for more than half the size of the [world economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy "World economy") for the next 1,500 years. In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"), the world economy slowly expanded with the increase of population and trade. During the early period of the Middle Ages, Europe was an economic backwater. However, by the later Medieval period, rich [trading cities in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_city-states "Italian city-states") emerged, creating the first modern [accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting "Accounting") and [finance systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_system "Financial system").

During the Industrial Revolution, [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth") in the modern sense first occurred during the Industrial Revolution in Britain and then in the rest of Europe due to high amounts of [energy conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conversion "Energy conversion"). Economic growth spread to all regions of the world during the twentieth century, when world GDP per capita quintupled. The highest growth occurred in the 1960s during post-war reconstruction. In particular, [shipping containers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipping_containers "Shipping containers") revolutionized trade in the second half of the century, by making it cheaper to transport goods, especially internationally. These gains have not been uniform across the globe; there are still many countries where people, especially young children, die from what are now preventable diseases, such as rotavirus and polio.

The [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession") happened from 2007 to 2009. Since 2020, economies have suffered from the [COVID-19 recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_recession "COVID-19 recession").
