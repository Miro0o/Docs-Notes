## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science

**Political science** (sometimes abbreviated as **PoliSci** or **poli sci**), also known as the academic field of **government** (**GOVT** or **Gov**), is the [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science") study of [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics "Politics") which is a [social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science") dealing with the [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis "Analysis") and [implementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implementation "Implementation") of systems of [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance "Governance") and its impact on [societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society "Society"). It deals with systems of [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance "Governance") and [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\) "Power (social and political)"), and the analysis of political activities, political thought, [political behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_behavior "Political behavior"), and associated [constitutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution") and [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law "Law"). Specialists in the field, most especially [subject-matter experts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subject-matter_expert "Subject-matter expert") are known as **political scientists**.

Unlike political philosophy alone, which is primarily [normative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normativity "Normativity") and evaluates politics through [value judgments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_judgments "Value judgments"), political science emphasizes descriptive and explanatory methods in analyzing phenomena and making policy decisions; it favors [empirical evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_evidence "Empirical evidence") over ethical judgments. Modern political science can generally be divided into the major sub-disciplines of [political philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy "Political philosophy"), [political methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_methodology "Political methodology"), [comparative politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_politics "Comparative politics"), [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations "International relations"), and [public policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy "Public policy") and [public administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration "Public administration"); though other smaller more specialized and niche subdisciplines exist as well.

Political science emerged as a distinct academic discipline in the late 19th century, influenced by the rise of [empiricism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism "Empiricism") and [positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivism "Positivism"). It emerged from history, philosophy, and law amid university professionalization and the founding of bodies such as the [American Political Science Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Political_Science_Association "American Political Science Association") in 1903. The [behavioral revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioralism "Behavioralism") of the 1950s played a significant role in shifting emphasis to scientifically explaining human behavior from an unbiased and neutral point of view using [quantitative research methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_research "Quantitative research") such as [surveys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_sampling "Survey sampling"), [historical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography "Historiography"), and empirical testing.
