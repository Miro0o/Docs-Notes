### What is Social Science?
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science

**Social science** (or the **social sciences**) is one of the [branches of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branches_of_science "Branches of science"), devoted to the study of [societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society "Society") and the [relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_relation "Social relation") among members within those societies. The term was formerly used to refer to the field of [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology "Sociology"), the original "science of society", established in the 18th century. It now encompasses a wide array of additional [academic disciplines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_discipline "Academic discipline"), including [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology"), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"), [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography "Geography"), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History"), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics "Linguistics"), [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management "Management"), [communication studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_studies "Communication studies"), [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology "Sociology"), [culturology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culturology "Culturology"), and [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science").

The majority of [positivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivism "Positivism") social scientists use methods resembling those used in the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science "Natural science") as tools for understanding societies, and so define science in its stricter [modern sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_science "Modern science"). Speculative social scientists, otherwise known as [interpretivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipositivism "Antipositivism") scientists, by contrast, may use social critique or symbolic interpretation rather than constructing [empirically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism "Empiricism") falsifiable theories, and thus treat science in its broader sense. In modern academic practice, researchers are often [eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism "Eclecticism"), using multiple [methodologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodology "Methodology") (combining both [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_research "Quantitative research") and [qualitative research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualitative_research "Qualitative research")). To gain a deeper understanding of complex human behavior in digital environments, social science disciplines have increasingly integrated interdisciplinary approaches, big data, and computational tools. The term [social research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_research "Social research") has also acquired a degree of autonomy as practitioners from various disciplines share similar goals and methods.
