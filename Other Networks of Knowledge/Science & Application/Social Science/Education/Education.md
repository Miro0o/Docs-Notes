## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education

**Education** is the transmission of [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge") and [skills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skill "Skill") and the development of [character traits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_trait "Character trait"). Formal education happens in a complex institutional framework, like public [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School "School"). [Non-formal education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-formal_education "Non-formal education") is also structured but occurs outside the formal schooling system, while [informal education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_education "Informal education") is unstructured learning through daily experiences. Formal and non-formal education are divided into levels that include [early childhood education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_childhood_education "Early childhood education"), [primary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education "Primary education"), [secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education "Secondary education"), and [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education "Tertiary education"). Other classifications focus on the teaching method, like teacher-centered and [student-centered education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student-centered_learning "Student-centered learning"), and on the subject, like [science education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_education "Science education"), [language education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_education "Language education"), and [physical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_education "Physical education"). The term "education" can also refer to the [mental states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_states "Mental states") and qualities of educated people and [the academic field studying educational phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_research "Educational research").

The precise definition of education is disputed, and there are disagreements about what the [aims of education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aims_of_education "Aims of education") are and to what extent education is different from [indoctrination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoctrination "Indoctrination") by fostering [critical thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thinking "Critical thinking"). These disagreements affect how to identify, measure, and improve forms of education. Fundamentally, education [socializes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialization "Socialization") children into society by [teaching cultural values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Values_education "Values education") and [norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_norm "Social norm"). It equips them with the skills needed to become productive members of society. This way, it stimulates [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth") and raises awareness of local and [global problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_global_issues "List of global issues"). Organized institutions affect many aspects of education. For example, governments set [education policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_policy "Education policy") to determine when school classes happen, [what is taught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum"), and who can or must attend. [International organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization "International organization"), like [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO"), have been influential in promoting [primary education for all children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Primary_Education "Universal Primary Education").

Many factors influence whether education is successful. [Psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology") factors include [motivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivation "Motivation"), [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence "Human intelligence"), and [personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality "Personality"). Social factors, like [socioeconomic status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_status "Socioeconomic status"), [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity "Ethnicity"), and [gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender "Gender"), are often linked to [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_in_education "Discrimination in education"). Further factors include access to [educational technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_technology "Educational technology"), teacher quality, and [parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parent "Parent") involvement.

The main academic field investigating education is called [education studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_studies "Education studies"). It examines what education is, what aims and effects it has, and how to improve it. Education studies has many subfields, like [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_education "Philosophy of education"), [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_of_education "Psychology of education"), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology_of_education "Sociology of education"), [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_anthropology "Educational anthropology"), and [economics of education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_of_education "Economics of education"). It also discusses [comparative education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_education "Comparative education"), [pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy "Pedagogy"), and the [history of education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_education "History of education").

In [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory "Prehistory"), education happened informally through [oral communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_communication "Oral communication") and [imitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imitation "Imitation"). With the rise of ancient [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilizations "Civilizations"), [writing was invented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention_of_writing "Invention of writing"), and the amount of knowledge grew. This caused a shift from informal to formal education. Initially, formal education was mainly available to elites and religious groups. The invention of the [printing press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press "Printing press") in the 15th century made books more widely available. This increased general [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy "Literacy"). Beginning in the 18th and 19th centuries, public education became more important. This development led to the worldwide process of making primary education available to all, [free of charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education "Free education"), and [compulsory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education "Compulsory education") up to a certain age. Today, over 90% of all primary-school-age children worldwide attend primary school.`
