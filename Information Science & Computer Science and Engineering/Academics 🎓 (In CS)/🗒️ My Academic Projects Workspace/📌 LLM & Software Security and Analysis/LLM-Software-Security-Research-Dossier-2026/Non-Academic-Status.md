## Current Capability Boundary As Of 2026-07-30

The current boundary is not "AI can hack" versus "AI cannot hack." The useful distinction is bounded, instrumented cyber reasoning versus open-world offensive reliability. Public evidence now supports the first claim strongly and the second claim only partially.

The short conclusion:

- AI is no longer only a coding or security assistant. Frontier agents can solve many CTF tasks, discover real vulnerabilities, validate exploitability in constrained environments, and sometimes construct working exploits.
- The strongest systems are hybrid cyber reasoning systems, not standalone chat sessions. They combine model reasoning with fuzzing, static analysis, symbolic or concolic execution, debuggers, build systems, sandboxed reproduction, and patch validation.
- The remaining boundary is reliability under real-world mess: long-horizon planning, incomplete setup, dependency and build failures, target-specific mitigations, false positives, stealth, changing environments, and adaptive defenders.

### CTF Capability

AI is already strong at CTFs below the elite tier. UK AISI reported that basic CTF-style cyber tasks had been saturated by frontier models, and that GPT-5.5 reached about 71.4% on expert-level cyber tasks covering exploitation, reverse engineering, crypto, and realistic synthetic vulnerabilities. In AISI's multi-step simulated corporate attack range, GPT-5.5 completed the range in 2 of 10 attempts and Anthropic Claude Mythos Preview completed it in 3 of 10 attempts. No evaluated model solved AISI's "Cooling Tower" industrial-control range.

Anthropic's 2025 competition writeup points in the same direction: Claude performed strongly in PicoCTF and Hack The Box AI-vs-human settings, but did not solve tasks in PlaidCTF or DEF CON CTF qualifiers. The practical conclusion is that current AI is good to excellent on beginner through many advanced CTF tasks, especially with scaffolding and tools, but it is not yet reliable at elite CTF difficulty.

Use this as a warning for benchmark interpretation: CTF success often reflects scaffold quality, tool access, time budget, parallelization, prompt elicitation, and task familiarity as much as raw model capability.

### Real Vulnerability Discovery

AI has become a serious vulnerability-discovery engine. AIxCC is the clearest public contest signal: finalist systems found and patched vulnerabilities across large C and Java codebases using CRS architectures rather than pure LLM prompting. The OpenSSF OSS-CRS transition is important because it moves AIxCC-style tooling toward reusable open-source security infrastructure.

Google Big Sleep and CodeMender are strong field signals from a major defender: they show AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and patching in real open-source software, using execution, static/dynamic analysis, fuzzing, SMT-style reasoning, critique agents, and human review. HackerOne and Bugcrowd signals also indicate that AI-assisted and partially autonomous bug finding is entering real bounty workflows.

The practical conclusion is that AI can find real vulnerabilities today, especially when a system can compile the target, run tests, fuzz, inspect traces, reason about sources/sinks, and validate candidate patches. The weaker claim is that a frontier model alone can inspect arbitrary production code and reliably find high-impact bugs. The literature and field reports do not support treating the model as a standalone vulnerability oracle.

### Exploit Construction

Exploit capability is real but uneven. ExploitBench is useful because it measures an exploitation ladder rather than a binary pass/fail score: reaching vulnerable code, reproducing crashes, constructing exploitation primitives, bypassing constraints, and reaching stronger control. Public ExploitBench/Bugcrowd reporting shows frontier cyber-capable models achieving meaningful progress on V8-style targets, with Claude Mythos Preview around the upper tier and GPT-5.5 variants behind it depending on setup.

The practical conclusion is that AI can often exploit known, seeded, or well-scaffolded vulnerabilities, and can sometimes help build real exploit chains. It is not yet consistently reliable across arbitrary modern targets with mitigations, custom allocators, brittle environment assumptions, version drift, kernel/browser hardening, and sparse feedback.

### Real-World Offensive Use

Threat-intelligence reporting shows that adversaries already use AI across reconnaissance, vulnerability research, exploit-development support, malware development, obfuscation, cloud/Kubernetes exploration, lateral-movement support, phishing, and operational automation. GTIG's May 2026 AI Threat Tracker is especially important because it reports a likely AI-developed zero-day exploit and broader movement from generic productivity toward vulnerability development and autonomous tool operation. Anthropic's GTG-1002 report is another strong vendor-side claim about agentic AI orchestration inside intrusion workflows.

Still, most real-world malicious use appears to be force multiplication rather than reliable press-button compromise. Humans remain important for target selection, infrastructure, operational security, validation, evasion, credential abuse, and deciding what to do when the environment diverges from the plan.

### Boundary Statement

AI has crossed the threshold for useful autonomous cyber reasoning in bounded environments. It is genuinely capable at CTFs, real bug discovery, triage, patching, and some exploit construction. The frontier boundary is open-world reliability: maintaining correct plans over long horizons, grounding results in executable evidence, handling messy infrastructure, hardening exploits, avoiding false positives, staying within disclosure and safety constraints, and operating against adaptive defenders.

### How To Use These Signals

- Separate benchmark success from real-world capability. A model solving an isolated range, CTF, or benchmark task is not proof that it can compromise a defended production target.
- Record model version, date, access mode, and scaffold. GPT-5.5 default, GPT-5.5 with TAC, GPT-5.5-Cyber, Claude Mythos Preview checkpoints, and Chinese models such as DeepSeek-R1/Qwen3/Doubao/ERNIE-style systems may differ because of access policy, context budget, tool scaffold, and refusal behavior.
- Prefer signals with reproducible environments, accepted bug reports, validated patches, or independent triage. Vendor claims are useful early warnings but should be labeled as vendor-reported until independently reproduced.
- For U.S.-China comparison, track both model capability and institutional deployment. U.S. signals currently cluster around AISI/METR/OpenAI/Anthropic/Google/AIxCC/OpenSSF/HackerOne/Bugcrowd/Palisade/Hack The Box; China signals cluster around CS-Eval, LiveSecBench, 天网杯, 阿里云 AI 安全赛事, 中国信通院/互联网协会 tracks, XCTF, and vendor white papers.
- Watch for benchmark leakage, prompt overfitting, and contest rules. CTF rankings can reflect agent scaffolding and human elicitation skill as much as raw model ability.
