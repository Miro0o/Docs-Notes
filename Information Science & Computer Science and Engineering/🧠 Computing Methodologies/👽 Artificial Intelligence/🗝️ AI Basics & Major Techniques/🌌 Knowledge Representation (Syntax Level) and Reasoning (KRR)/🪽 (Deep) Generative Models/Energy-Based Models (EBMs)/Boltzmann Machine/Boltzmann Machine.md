## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_machine

A **Boltzmann machine** (also called **Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model with external field** or **stochastic Ising model**), named after [Ludwig Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Boltzmann "Ludwig Boltzmann"), is a [spin-glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glass "Spin glass") model with an external field, i.e., a [Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glass#Sherrington%E2%80%93Kirkpatrick_model "Spin glass"),[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_machine#cite_note-1) that is a stochastic [Ising model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ising_model "Ising model"). It is a [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics "Statistical physics") technique applied in the context of [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science "Cognitive science").[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_machine#cite_note-:0-2) It is also classified as a [Markov random field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_random_field "Markov random field").[3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_machine#cite_note-3)

Boltzmann machines are theoretically intriguing because of the locality and [Hebbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebbian "Hebbian") nature of their training algorithm (being trained by Hebb's rule), and because of their [parallelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelism_\(computing\) "Parallelism (computing)") and the resemblance of their dynamics to simple [physical processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_process "Physical process"). Boltzmann machines with unconstrained connectivity have not been proven useful for practical problems in [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning") or [inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference "Inference"), but if the connectivity is properly constrained, the learning can be made efficient enough to be useful for practical problems.[4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_machine#cite_note-4)

They are named after the [Boltzmann distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_distribution "Boltzmann distribution") in [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics"), which is used in their [sampling function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_function "Sampling function"). They were heavily popularized and promoted by [Geoffrey Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Hinton "Geoffrey Hinton"), [Terry Sejnowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Sejnowski "Terry Sejnowski") and [Yann LeCun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun "Yann LeCun") in cognitive sciences communities, particularly in [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning"),[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_machine#cite_note-:0-2) as part of "[energy-based models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy-based_model "Energy-based model")" (EBM), because [Hamiltonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_function "Hamiltonian function") of [spin glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glasses "Spin glasses") as energy are used as a starting point to define the learning task.
