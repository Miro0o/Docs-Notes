## Intro
In neural networks, optimizers are algorithms that derive the best set of parameter values that minimize the loss function (see later).

Table shows a small set of optimizers and their characteristics – No straightforward way to choose!

| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gradient Descent (GD)                                           | 1847 | Cauchy (1847), *Méthode générale pour la résolution des systèmes d'équations simultanées*                                                        | $g_t=\nabla_\theta L(\theta_t)$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta g_t$                                                                                                                                                                                                            | The fundamental first-order optimization algorithm. Uses the full gradient of the objective function to update parameters.                  |
| Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD)                               | 1951 | Robbins & Monro (1951), *A Stochastic Approximation Method*; Rumelhart et al. (1986), *Learning representations by back-propagating errors*      | $g_t=\nabla_\theta L(\theta_t;\xi_t)$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta g_t$                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Uses mini-batch stochastic gradients instead of full gradients, enabling efficient training of large neural networks.                       |
| Heavy Ball Momentum                                             | 1964 | Polyak (1964), *Some methods of speeding up the convergence of iteration methods*                                                                | $v_t=\beta v_{t-1}+g_t$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta v_t$                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Introduces momentum by accumulating previous gradients, accelerating convergence and reducing oscillations.                                 |
| Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG)                             | 1983 | Nesterov (1983), *A method for solving a convex programming problem with convergence rate O(1/k²)*                                               | $\tilde{\theta}_t=\theta_t-\eta\beta v_{t-1}$ <br> $v_t=\beta v_{t-1}+\nabla L(\tilde{\theta}_t)$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta v_t$                                                                                                                                          | Improves momentum by computing gradients at a predicted future position, achieving faster convergence.                                      |
| Adagrad                                                         | 2011 | Duchi, Hazan & Singer (2011), *Adaptive Subgradient Methods for Online Learning and Stochastic Optimization*                                     | $G_t=\sum_{i=1}^{t}g_i^2$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{G_t+\epsilon}}g_t$                                                                                                                                                                                       | Adjusts learning rates independently for each parameter based on historical gradient magnitudes.                                            |
| RMSprop                                                         | 2012 | Hinton (2012), *Neural Networks for Machine Learning Lecture 6.5*                                                                                | $v_t=\rho v_{t-1}+(1-\rho)g_t^2$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{g_t}{\sqrt{v_t+\epsilon}}$                                                                                                                                                                                | Uses exponential moving averages of squared gradients to avoid Adagrad's overly aggressive learning-rate decay.                             |
| Adadelta                                                        | 2012 | Zeiler (2012), *ADADELTA: An Adaptive Learning Rate Method*                                                                                      | $E[g^2]_t=\rho E[g^2]_{t-1}+(1-\rho)g_t^2$ <br> $RMS[g]_t=\sqrt{E[g^2]_t+\epsilon}$ <br> $\Delta\theta_t=-\frac{RMS[\Delta\theta]_{t-1}}{RMS[g]_t}g_t$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\Delta\theta_t$                                                                               | Extends Adagrad by limiting the accumulation window and reducing sensitivity to learning-rate selection.                                    |
| Adam                                                            | 2014 | Kingma & Ba (2014), *Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization*                                                                                 | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ <br> $\hat m_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t}$ <br> $\hat v_t=\frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$                                        | Combines momentum and adaptive learning rates through first- and second-order moment estimation. The most widely used adaptive optimizer.   |
| Adamax                                                          | 2015 | Kingma & Ba (2014), *Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization*                                                                                 | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $\hat{m}_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t}$ <br> $u_t=\max(\beta_2u_{t-1},\|g_t\|)$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat{m}_t}{u_t}$                                                                                                      | An infinity-norm variant of Adam that improves numerical stability by replacing the second moment estimate with an infinity norm.           |
| Nadam                                                           | 2016 | Dozat (2016), *Incorporating Nesterov Momentum into Adam*                                                                                        | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ <br> $\hat m_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t}$ <br> $\hat v_t=\frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\beta_1\hat m_t+(1-\beta_1)g_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$                  | Combines Adam's adaptive moment estimation with Nesterov momentum acceleration.                                                             |
| AdamW                                                           | 2017 | Loshchilov & Hutter (2017), *Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization*                                                                              | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}-\eta\lambda\theta_t$                                                                                                    | Decouples weight decay from gradient updates, improving generalization compared with L2 regularization in Adam.                             |
| LARS (Layer-wise Adaptive Rate Scaling)                         | 2017 | You et al. (2017), *Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks*                                                                              | $g_t=\nabla_\theta L(\theta_t)$ <br> $\eta_l=\eta\frac{\|\theta_l\|}{\|g_l\|+\epsilon}$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_l g_t$                                                                                                                                                  | A layer-wise adaptive optimizer designed for very large batch training. It scales learning rates according to parameter and gradient norms. |
| AMSGrad                                                         | 2018 | Reddi, Kale & Kumar (2018), *On the Convergence of Adam and Beyond*                                                                              | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ <br> $\hat v_t=\max(\hat v_{t-1},v_t)$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$                                                                                      | Modifies Adam by enforcing a non-increasing second-moment estimate, improving convergence guarantees.                                       |
| Yogi                                                            | 2018 | Zaheer et al. (2018), *Adaptive Methods for Nonconvex Optimization*                                                                              | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=v_{t-1}-(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{sign}(v_{t-1}-g_t^2)g_t^2$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{m_t}{\sqrt{v_t}+\epsilon}$                                                                                                       | A modification of Adam that controls the growth of second moments, preventing overly aggressive adaptive learning rates.                    |
| Adafactor                                                       | 2018 | Shazeer & Stern (2018), *Adafactor: Adaptive Learning Rates with Sublinear Memory Cost*                                                          | $V_t\approx R_tC_t$ <br> $R_t=\rho R_{t-1}+(1-\rho)G_t^2$ <br> $C_t=\rho C_{t-1}+(1-\rho)G_t^2$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{g_t}{\sqrt{V_t}+\epsilon}$                                                                                                                 | A memory-efficient Adam variant that factorizes second-moment statistics, making it suitable for very large models.                         |
| LAMB (Layer-wise Adaptive Moments optimizer for Batch training) | 2019 | You et al. (2019), *Large Batch Optimization for Deep Learning: Training BERT in 76 minutes*                                                     | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ <br> $r_t=\frac{\|\theta_t\|}{\|\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}\|}$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta r_t\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$                                      | Extends Adam with layer-wise trust ratios, enabling stable large-batch training of transformer models such as BERT.                         |
| NovoGrad                                                        | 2019 | Ginsburg et al. (2019), *Stochastic Gradient Methods with Layer-wise Adaptive Moments for Training of Deep Networks*                             | $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\|g_t\|^2$ <br> $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+\frac{g_t}{\sqrt{v_t}+\epsilon}$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta m_t$                                                                                                                                        | A layer-wise adaptive optimizer designed for efficient training of large-scale vision models.                                               |
| AdaBound                                                        | 2019 | Luo et al. (2019), *Adaptive Gradient Methods with Dynamic Bound of Learning Rate*                                                               | Adam update: <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$ <br> $\eta_t$ is dynamically clipped: <br> $l_t\leq\eta_t\leq u_t$                                                                                                                     | Combines Adam's fast convergence with SGD's generalization by gradually bounding adaptive learning rates.                                   |
| Lookahead                                                       | 2019 | Zhang et al. (2019), *Lookahead Optimizer: k steps forward, 1 step back*                                                                         | Fast weights: <br> $w_f^{t+k}=Optimizer(w_f^t)$ <br> Slow weights update: <br> $w_s^{t+1}=w_s^t+\alpha(w_f^{t+k}-w_s^t)$ <br> $w_f^{t+k}\leftarrow w_s^{t+1}$                                                                                                                    | Maintains fast and slow weights. The slow weights stabilize optimization trajectories and improve convergence.                              |
| RAdam                                                           | 2019 | Liu et al. (2019), *On the Variance of the Adaptive Learning Rate and Beyond*                                                                    | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ <br> $\rho_t=\rho_\infty-\frac{2t\beta_2^t}{1-\beta_2^t}$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta r_t\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$                                                          | Rectifies the variance of Adam's adaptive learning rate in early training stages, reducing dependence on warm-up.                           |
| Ranger                                                          | 2019 | Wright (2019), *Ranger - a synergistic optimizer combining RAdam with LookAhead*                                                                 | RAdam update: <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta r_t\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}$ <br> Lookahead update: <br> $w_s\leftarrow w_s+\alpha(w_f-w_s)$                                                                                                                     | Combines RAdam and Lookahead to improve stability and convergence.                                                                          |
| AdaBelief                                                       | 2020 | Zhuang et al. (2020), *AdaBelief Optimizer: Adapting Stepsizes by the Belief in Observed Gradients*                                              | $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ <br> $s_t=\beta_2s_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)(g_t-m_t)^2$ <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{m_t}{\sqrt{s_t}+\epsilon}$                                                                                                                            | Adapts learning rates according to the uncertainty of gradient estimates, improving convergence and generalization.                         |
| SAM (Sharpness-Aware Minimization)                              | 2020 | Foret et al. Improves generalization.                   |
| Ranger21                                                        | 2021 | Wright & Demeure (2021), *Ranger21: A Synergistic Deep Learning Optimizer*                                                                       | Combination of: <br> AdamW update <br> Lookahead interpolation <br> Gradient centralization <br> Adaptive gradient clipping                                                                                                                                                      | A hybrid optimizer integrating several modern optimization techniques for robust deep learning training.                                    |
| ASAM                                                            | 2021 | Kwon et al. | MADGRAD                                                         | 2021 | Defazio & Jelassi (2021), *Adaptivity without Compromise: A Momentumized, Adaptive, Dual Averaged Gradient Method for Large Scale Deep Learning* | Accumulated gradient: <br> $s_t=s_{t-1}+g_t$ <br> Dual averaging variable: <br> $z_t=z_{t-1}+g_t$ <br> Parameter update: <br> $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{z_t}{(s_t+\epsilon)^{1/3}}$                                                                                       | A momentumized adaptive dual averaging optimizer combining advantages of SGD and adaptive methods.                                          |
| Ranger21                                                        | 2021 | Wright & Demeure (2021), *Ranger21: A Synergistic Deep Learning Optimizer*                                                                       | Combines: <br> AdamW update <br> Lookahead interpolation <br> Gradient centralization <br> Adaptive gradient clipping <br> Stable weight decay                                                                                                                                   | A hybrid optimizer integrating multiple optimization improvements into a single framework.                                                  |
| ASAM                                                            | 2021 | Kwon et al. (2021), *ASAM: Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization for Scale-Invariant Learning of Deep Neural Networks*                           | Perturbation: <br> $\epsilon^*=\rho\frac{D^2\nabla L(w)}{\|D\nabla L(w)\|}$ <br> Update: <br> $w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\nabla L(w_t+\epsilon^*)$                                                                                                                                         | Adaptive version of SAM that improves robustness by making sharpness measurement scale invariant.                                           |
| Adan                                                            | 2022 | Xie et al. | Lion                                                            | 2023 | Chen et al. | LionW                                                           | 2023 | Chen et al.
