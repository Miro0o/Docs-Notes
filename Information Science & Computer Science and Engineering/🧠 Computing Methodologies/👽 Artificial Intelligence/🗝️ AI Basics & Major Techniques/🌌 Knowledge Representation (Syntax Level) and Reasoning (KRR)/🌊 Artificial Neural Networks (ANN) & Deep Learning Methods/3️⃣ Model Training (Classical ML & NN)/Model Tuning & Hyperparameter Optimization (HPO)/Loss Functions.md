## Intro
> 🤖 GPT5.6-Sol

A **loss** is the scalar minimized during learning. An **objective** may combine several losses, rewards, constraints, and regularizers. This distinction matters: SimCLR and MoCo use variants of InfoNCE; DDIM is primarily a sampler; RLHF is a pipeline; and PPO/GRPO are policy-optimization objectives rather than standalone supervised losses.

The catalog below includes canonical families and historically influential named objectives. It is deliberately not a list of every paper-specific reweighting or renamed variant. Dates refer to the cited formulation or its influential machine-learning use, not necessarily the first appearance of the underlying mathematics.

│   ├── Contrastive

│   ├── Redundancy reduction and clustering

│
├── 4. Generative-model objectives
│   ├── Autoencoder and VAE
│   ├── GAN
│   ├── Energy-based model and normalizing flow
│   ├── Diffusion and score matching
│   └── Flow matching, rectified flow, and consistency
│
├── 5. Vision losses
│   ├── Segmentation and overlap
│   ├── Detection and localization

├── 6. NLP and sequence losses
│   ├── Token/language modeling

### Detailed catalog
Notation: $z$ denotes logits, $p$ a predicted distribution, $y$ a target, $e=y-\hat y$, $\sigma$ the logistic sigmoid, $d$ a distance, $s$ a similarity, $\tau$ a temperature, and $\operatorname{sg}$ stop-gradient. Constants and averaging factors are omitted when they do not change the objective.

| Classical supervised           | Regression                       | **Mean absolute error (MAE, L1)**                                  |      1757 | $\mathcal L=\frac1N\sum_i\lVert y_i-\hat y_i\rVert_1$                                                                              | Robust to large residuals relative to MSE; nondifferentiable at zero.                                                 | Boscovich (1757), early least-absolute-deviation method                                                               |
|                                | Regression                       | **Least squares / mean squared error (MSE, L2)**                   |      1805 | $\mathcal L=\frac1N\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2$                                                                                         | Gaussian conditional-mean estimator; strongly penalizes outliers.                                                     | [Legendre (1805)](https://archive.org/details/nouvellesmthode00legegoog)                                              |
|                                | Probabilistic                    | **Maximum likelihood / negative log-likelihood (NLL)**             |      1922 | $\mathcal L=-\sum_i\log p_\theta(y_i\mid x_i)$                                                                                     | General family, not a separate loss from categorical CE or Gaussian NLL when those likelihoods are chosen.            | [Fisher (1922)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1922.0009)                                                               |
|                                | Probabilistic                    | **Brier score**                                                    |      1950 | $\mathcal L=\sum_k(p_k-y_k)^2$                                                                                                     | Proper scoring rule for probabilistic classification; directly measures calibration and accuracy.                     | [Brier (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078%3C0001:VOFEIT%3E2.0.CO;2)                                   |

|                                | Classification                   | **Perceptron criterion**                                           |      1958 | $\mathcal L=\sum_i\max(0,-y_i f(x_i))$                                                                                             | Penalizes misclassified examples but imposes no unit margin.                                                          | [Rosenblatt (1958)](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042519)                                                                 |

|                                | Robust regression                | **Huber loss**                                                     |      1964 | $\ell_\delta(e)=\frac12e^2$ if $\lvert e\rvert\le\delta$; else $\delta(\lvert e\rvert-\frac12\delta)$                              | Quadratic near zero and linear in the tails.                                                                          | [Huber (1964)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732)                                                               |
|                                | Count regression                 | **Poisson NLL / Poisson deviance**                                 |      1972 | $\mathcal L_{NLL}=\hat\lambda-y\log\hat\lambda$; $D=2[y\log(y/\hat\lambda)-y+\hat\lambda]$                                         | Appropriate to nonnegative count targets; NLL and deviance differ only by a target-dependent constant.                | [Nelder & Wedderburn (1972)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2344614)                                                         |
|                                | Regression                       | **Quantile (pinball) loss**                                        |      1978 | $\rho_\tau(e)=\max(\tau e,(\tau-1)e)$                                                                                              | Estimates a conditional quantile rather than the conditional mean.                                                    | [Koenker & Bassett (1978)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913643)                                                           |
|                                | Classification                   | **Categorical / softmax cross-entropy**                            |     1980s | $p_k=\operatorname{softmax}(z)_k,\quad\mathcal L=-\sum_k y_k\log p_k$                                                              | Categorical NLL; Shannon introduced entropy, not the modern neural-network loss itself.                               | [Bridle (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-76153-9_28)                                                         |
|                                | Probabilistic regression         | **Gaussian NLL**                                                   |      1994 | $\mathcal L=\frac12[\log\sigma^2(x)+(y-\mu(x))^2/\sigma^2(x)]$                                                                     | Learns input-dependent mean and variance; fixed variance reduces it to scaled MSE plus a constant.                    | [Nix & Weigend (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICNN.1994.374138)                                                      |

### Important equivalences and non-equivalences
- **MLE and NLL:** maximizing likelihood is exactly minimizing NLL.
- **Cross-entropy and NLL:** for one-hot labels, categorical cross-entropy is categorical NLL; binary cross-entropy is Bernoulli NLL.
- **MSE and Gaussian NLL:** MSE is proportional to Gaussian NLL only when the conditional variance is fixed.
- **Contrastive names:** CPC/InfoNCE, SimCLR/NT-Xent, and MoCo’s queue-based objective belong to one broad softmax-contrastive family, although their sampling and encoders differ.
- **Smooth L1 and Huber:** Smooth L1 is a scaled/parameterized Huber loss, not an unrelated historical loss.
- **DDIM:** DDIM changes the diffusion generative process/sampler and normally uses a DDPM-style trained network; “DDIM loss” should not be listed as a canonical separate loss.
- **Frameworks versus losses:** RLHF, RLVR, SimCLR, MoCo, and many multimodal systems are pipelines or methods. Their actual optimized objectives are the component losses shown above.
- **Composite objectives:** practical systems commonly minimize $\sum_j\lambda_j\mathcal L_j$. DETR, BLIP, VAEs, RLHF, and GRPO are examples in which calling the whole expression a single “loss function” can hide important components.
