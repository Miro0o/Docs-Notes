## Intro
> 🤖 GPT5.6-Sol

An **optimizer** is an update rule (and its state) used to change model parameters so as to reduce an objective. A learning-rate schedule, weight-decay rule, gradient transformation, state-sharding system, or parameter-averaging method can modify optimization without being a standalone optimizer. This distinction matters: AdamW is Adam with decoupled weight decay; Lookahead and SAM wrap a base optimizer; GaLore projects gradients; and ZeRO partitions state rather than defining a new mathematical update.

The catalog below includes canonical optimization families and historically influential deep-learning methods. It does not include every paper-specific combination or implementation alias. Dates refer to the cited formulation or its influential neural-network use; update rules are schematic and omit implementation details such as bias correction where noted.


### Taxonomy and summary

```text

│
├── 1. Classical deterministic and stochastic methods
│   ├── Gradient and stochastic-gradient descent
│   ├── Momentum and acceleration
│   ├── Conjugate-gradient and quasi-Newton methods
│   └── Resilient backpropagation
│
├── 2. Diagonal adaptive first-order methods
│   ├── Accumulated and exponential second moments
│   ├── Adam family
│   └── Convergence and generalization variants
│
├── 3. Layer-wise and large-batch methods
│   ├── Trust-ratio scaling
│   └── Layer-wise moment estimation
│
├── 4. Curvature-aware and matrix-preconditioned methods
│   ├── Natural-gradient and Hessian approximations
│   ├── Kronecker and matrix preconditioning
│   └── Orthogonalized matrix updates
│
├── 5. Memory-efficient large-model methods
│   ├── Factored or grouped optimizer state
│   ├── Quantized optimizer state
│   └── Low-rank gradient projection
│
├── 6. Meta-optimizers and geometry/generalization methods
│   ├── Weight interpolation and averaging
│   ├── Sharpness-aware optimization
│   ├── Sign and gradient-difference methods
│   └── Learning-rate-free/schedule-free methods
│
└── 7. Distributed and federated optimization
    ├── Local/model averaging
    ├── Sign-compressed updates
    └── Low-rank gradient communication
```

### Important equivalences and non-equivalences
- **SGD terminology:** in deep learning, “SGD” usually means mini-batch stochastic gradient descent; full-batch gradient descent is different computationally and statistically.
- **Momentum conventions:** PyTorch-style velocity, exponentially weighted gradients, and Nesterov formulations can look different while producing closely related updates after reparameterization.
- **Adam and AdamW:** adding $\lambda\lVert\theta\rVert^2$ to the loss is not generally equivalent to decoupled weight decay under an adaptive preconditioner.
- **RMSProp and Adam:** Adam is not simply “RMSProp with momentum”; bias correction and the precise moment coupling matter.
- **Optimizer versus schedule:** warm-up, cosine decay, one-cycle schedules, and gradient clipping materially affect training but are not optimizers.
- **Optimizer versus wrapper:** Lookahead, SAM/ASAM, GaLore, SWA, mixed precision, and gradient accumulation modify a base optimization process.
- **Optimizer versus distributed system:** ZeRO/FSDP shard states and parameters; FedAvg and PowerSGD specify communication/aggregation around local optimization.
- **First- versus second-order:** Sophia, K-FAC, Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon use different curvature or matrix geometry; calling all of them “second-order” hides substantial differences.
- **No universally best optimizer:** architecture, normalization, batch size, sparsity, precision, schedule, and tuning budget can change the ranking. AdamW is a common Transformer default; momentum SGD remains a strong vision/generalization baseline.
