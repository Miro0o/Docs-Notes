## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verifiable_computing

**Verifiable computing** (or **verified computation** or **verified computing**) enables a [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer "Computer") to [offload](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation_offloading "Computation offloading") the [computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation "Computation") of some function, to other perhaps untrusted [clients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client%E2%80%93server_model "Client–server model"), while maintaining verifiable results. The other clients evaluate the function and return the result with a proof that the [computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation "Computation") of the function was carried out correctly. The introduction of this notion came as a result of the increasingly common phenomenon of "[outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outsourcing "Outsourcing")" computation to untrusted users in projects such as [SETI@home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SETI@home "SETI@home") and also to the growing desire to enable computationally-weak devices to outsource computational tasks to a more powerful computation service, as in [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing "Cloud computing"). The concept dates back to work by Babai et al.,[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verifiable_computing#cite_note-1) and has been studied under various terms, including "checking computations" (Babai et al.), "delegating computations",[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verifiable_computing#cite_note-2) "certified computation",[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verifiable_computing#cite_note-:0-3) and verifiable computing. The term _verifiable computing_ itself was formalized by Rosario Gennaro, [Craig Gentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Gentry_\(computer_scientist\) "Craig Gentry (computer scientist)"), and Bryan Parno, and echoes Micali's "certified computation".
