## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_calculus

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), the **process calculi** (or **process algebras**) are a diverse family of related approaches for formally modelling [concurrent systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_system "Concurrent system"). Process calculi provide tools for high-level descriptions of interactions, communications, and synchronizations between a collection of independent processes. They provide [algebraic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra") laws that allow process descriptions to be manipulated and analyzed, and they also permit formal reasoning about equivalences between processes (e.g., using [bisimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisimulation "Bisimulation")). Leading examples of process calculi include [CSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communicating_Sequential_Processes "Communicating Sequential Processes"), [CCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_Communicating_Systems "Calculus of Communicating Systems"), [ACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_of_Communicating_Processes "Algebra of Communicating Processes"), and [LOTOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_Of_Temporal_Ordering_Specification "Language Of Temporal Ordering Specification"). More recent additions to the family include the [π-calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi-calculus "Pi-calculus"), the [ambient calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_calculus "Ambient calculus"), [PEPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEPA "PEPA"), the [fusion calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_calculus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Fusion calculus (page does not exist)") and the [join-calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join-calculus "Join-calculus").
