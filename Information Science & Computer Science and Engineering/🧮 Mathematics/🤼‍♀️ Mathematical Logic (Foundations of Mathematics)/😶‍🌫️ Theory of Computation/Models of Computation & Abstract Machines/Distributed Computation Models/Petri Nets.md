## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petri_net

A **Petri net**, also known as a **place/transition net** (**PT net**), is one of several [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical "Mathematical") [modeling languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modeling_language "Modeling language") for the description of [distributed systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_systems "Distributed systems"). It is a class of [discrete event dynamic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_event_dynamic_system "Discrete event dynamic system"). A Petri net is a directed [bipartite graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartite_graph "Bipartite graph") that has two types of elements: places and transitions. Place elements are depicted as white circles and transition elements are depicted as rectangles. A place can contain any number of tokens, depicted as black circles. A transition is enabled if all places connected to it as inputs contain at least one token. Like industry standards such as [UML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Modeling_Language "Unified Modeling Language") [activity diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activity_diagram "Activity diagram"), [Business Process Model and Notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Process_Model_and_Notation "Business Process Model and Notation"), and [event-driven process chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event-driven_process_chain "Event-driven process chain"), Petri nets offer a [graphical notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagram "Diagram") for stepwise processes that include choice, [iteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iteration "Iteration"), and [concurrent execution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_computing "Concurrent computing"). Unlike these standards, Petri nets have an exact mathematical definition of their execution semantics, with a well-developed mathematical theory for process analysis.
