## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_%CE%BC-calculus

In [theoretical computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_science "Theoretical computer science"), the **modal μ-calculus** (**Lμ**, **Lμ**, or **propositional mu-calculus**, sometimes just **μ-calculus**, although this can have a more general meaning) is an extension of [propositional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_logic "Propositional logic") [modal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_logic "Modal logic") (with [many modalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimodal_logic "Multimodal logic")) by adding the [least fixed point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_fixed_point "Least fixed point") operator μ and the [greatest fixed point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_fixed_point "Greatest fixed point") operator ν, thus a [fixed-point logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-point_logic "Fixed-point logic").

The (propositional, modal) μ-calculus originates with [Dana Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Scott "Dana Scott") and [Jaco de Bakker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaco_de_Bakker "Jaco de Bakker"), and was further developed by [Dexter Kozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dexter_Kozen "Dexter Kozen") into the version most used nowadays. It is used to describe properties of [labelled transition systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labelled_transition_system "Labelled transition system") and for [verifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_checking "Model checking") these properties. Many [temporal logics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_logic "Temporal logic") can be encoded in the μ-calculus, including [CTL*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTL* "CTL*") and its widely used fragments—[linear temporal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_temporal_logic "Linear temporal logic") and [computational tree logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_tree_logic "Computational tree logic").

An algebraic view is to see it as an [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_algebra "Universal algebra") of [monotonic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonic_function "Monotonic function") over a [complete lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_lattice "Complete lattice"), with operators consisting of [functional composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_composition "Functional composition") plus the least and greatest fixed point operators; from this viewpoint, the modal μ-calculus is over the lattice of a [power set algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_set_algebra "Power set algebra"). The [game semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_semantics "Game semantics") of μ-calculus is related to [two-player games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-player_game "Two-player game") with [perfect information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_information "Perfect information"), particularly infinite [parity games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_game "Parity game").
