## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_geometry

In mathematics, **arithmetic geometry** is roughly the application of techniques from [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry") to problems in [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"). Arithmetic geometry is centered around [Diophantine geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_geometry "Diophantine geometry"), the study of [rational points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_point "Rational point") of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_variety)

In more abstract terms, arithmetic geometry can be defined as the study of [schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_\(mathematics\) "Scheme (mathematics)") of [finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_morphism#Morphisms_of_finite_type "Finite morphism") over the [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_of_a_ring "Spectrum of a ring") of the [ring of integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_integers)

The classical objects of interest in arithmetic geometry are rational points: [sets of solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solution_set "Solution set") of a [system of polynomial equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_polynomial_equations "System of polynomial equations") over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field "Number field"), [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field "Finite field"), [p-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field "P-adic field"), or [function fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_function_field "Algebraic function field"), i.e. [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_\(mathematics\) "Field (mathematics)") that are not [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed "Algebraically closed") excluding the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_number "Real number"). Rational points can be directly characterized by [height functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_function "Height function") which measure their arithmetic complexity.

The structure of algebraic varieties defined over non-algebraically closed fields has become a central area of interest that arose with the modern abstract development of algebraic geometry. Over finite fields, [étale cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tale_cohomology "Étale cohomology") provides [topological invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_property "Topological property") associated to algebraic varieties. [p-adic Hodge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_Hodge_theory "P-adic Hodge theory") gives tools to examine when cohomological properties of varieties over the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_number "Complex number") extend to those over [p-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field "P-adic field")

> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_of_numbers

**Geometry of numbers**, also known as **geometric number theory**, is the part of [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory") which uses [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") for the study of [algebraic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number). Typically, a [ring of algebraic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_algebraic_integers "Ring of algebraic integers") is viewed as a [lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_\(group\) "Lattice (group)") in $\mathbb {R} ^{n}$ and the study of these lattices provides fundamental information on algebraic numbers. [Hermann Minkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Minkowski "Hermann Minkowski") ([1896](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_of_numbers#https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~b3tran/cgm/Minkowski_SpaceAndTime_1909.pdf)) initiated this line of research at the age of 26 in his work _The Geometry of Numbers_.

The geometry of numbers has a close relationship with other fields of mathematics, especially [functional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_analysis "Functional analysis") and [Diophantine approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_approximation "Diophantine approximation"), the problem of finding [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_number "Rational number") that approximate an [irrational quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_number "Irrational number").
