## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternion#

In [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), the **quaternion** [number system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_system "Number system") extends the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_number "Complex number"). Quaternions were first described by the Irish mathematician [William Rowan Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rowan_Hamilton "William Rowan Hamilton") in 1843 and applied to [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") in [three-dimensional space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional_space "Three-dimensional space"). The set of all quaternions is conventionally denoted by  $\mathbb {H}$  ('H' for _Hamilton_) or by **H**. Quaternions are not a [_field_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_\(mathematics\) "Field (mathematics)") because multiplication of quaternions is not [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative "Commutative"). Quaternions provide a definition of the quotient of two [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_\(mathematics_and_physics\) "Vector (mathematics and physics)") in a three-dimensional space. Quaternions are generally represented in the form $$\ a+b\,\mathbf {i} +c\,\mathbf {j} +d\,\mathbf {k} \ $$
where the coefficients a, b, c, d are [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_number "Real number"), and 1, **i**, **j**, **k** are the _basis vectors_ or _basis elements_.

Quaternions are used in [pure mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_mathematics "Pure mathematics"), but also have practical uses in [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics"), particularly for [calculations involving three-dimensional rotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions_and_spatial_rotation "Quaternions and spatial rotation"), such as in [three-dimensional computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics "3D computer graphics"), [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision "Computer vision"), robotics, [magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging") and [crystallographic texture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texture_\(crystalline\) "Texture (crystalline)") analysis.They can be used alongside other methods of rotation, such as [Euler angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_angles "Euler angles") and [rotation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_matrix "Rotation matrix"), or as an alternative to them, depending on the application.

In modern terms, quaternions form a four-dimensional [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_algebra "Associative algebra") [normed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_algebra "Composition algebra") [division algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_algebra "Division algebra") over the real numbers, and therefore a ring, also a [division ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_ring "Division ring") and a [domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_\(ring_theory\) "Domain (ring theory)"). It is a special case of a [Clifford algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_algebra "Clifford algebra"), [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_Clifford_algebras "Classification of Clifford algebras") as $\operatorname {Cl} _{0,2}(\mathbb {R} )\cong \operatorname {Cl} _{3,0}^{+}(\mathbb {R} )$ It was the first noncommutative division algebra to be discovered.

According to the [Frobenius theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_theorem_\(real_division_algebras\) "Frobenius theorem (real division algebras)"), the algebra H![{\displaystyle \mathbb {H} }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/e050965453c42bcc6bd544546703c836bdafeac9) is one of only two finite-dimensional [division rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_ring "Division ring") containing a proper [subring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subring "Subring") [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism "Isomorphism") to the real numbers; the other being the complex numbers. These rings are also [Euclidean Hurwitz algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz%27s_theorem_\(composition_algebras\) "Hurwitz's theorem (composition algebras)"), of which the quaternions are the largest [associative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_algebra "Associative algebra") (and hence the largest ring). Further extending the quaternions yields the [non-associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-associative_algebra "Non-associative algebra") [octonions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octonion "Octonion"), which is the last [normed division algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_division_algebra "Normed division algebra") over the real numbers. The next extension gives the [sedenions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedenions "Sedenions"), which have [zero divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_divisor "Zero divisor") and so cannot be a normed division algebra.

The [unit quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_quaternion "Unit quaternion") give a [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_\(mathematics\) "Group (mathematics)") structure on the [3-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-sphere "3-sphere") S3 isomorphic to the groups [Spin(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin\(3\) "Spin(3)") and [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU\(2\) "SU(2)"), i.e. the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover "Universal cover") group of [SO(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO\(3\) "SO(3)"). The positive and negative basis vectors form the eight-element [quaternion group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternion_group "Quaternion group").
