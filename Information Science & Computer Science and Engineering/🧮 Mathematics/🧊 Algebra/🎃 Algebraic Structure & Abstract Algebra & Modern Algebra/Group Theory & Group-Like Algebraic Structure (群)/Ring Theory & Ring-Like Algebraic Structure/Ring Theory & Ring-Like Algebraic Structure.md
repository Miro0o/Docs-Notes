## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiring

In [abstract algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra"), a **semiring** is an [algebraic structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_structure "Algebraic structure"). Semirings are a generalization of [rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_\(algebra\) "Ring (algebra)"), dropping the requirement that each element must have an [additive inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_inverse "Additive inverse"). At the same time, semirings are a generalization of [bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_\(order\)#Bounded_lattice "Lattice (order)") [distributive lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributive_lattice "Distributive lattice").

The smallest semiring that is not a ring is the [two-element Boolean algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-element_Boolean_algebra "Two-element Boolean algebra"), for instance with [logical disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_disjunction "Logical disjunction") ∨![{\displaystyle \lor }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ab47f6b1f589aedcf14638df1d63049d233d851a) as addition. A motivating example that is neither a ring nor a lattice is the set of [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_number "Natural number") N![{\displaystyle \mathbb {N} }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/fdf9a96b565ea202d0f4322e9195613fb26a9bed) (including zero) under ordinary addition and multiplication. Semirings are abundant because a suitable multiplication operation arises as the [function composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_composition "Function composition") of [endomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endomorphism "Endomorphism") over any [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid "Commutative monoid").
