## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_theory

In [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), **trace theory** aims to provide a concrete mathematical underpinning for the study of [concurrent computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_computation "Concurrent computation") and [process calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_calculi "Process calculi"). The underpinning is provided by an [algebraic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra") definition of the [free partially commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_partially_commutative_monoid "Free partially commutative monoid") or [trace monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_monoid "Trace monoid"), or equivalently, the [history monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_monoid "History monoid"), which provides a concrete algebraic foundation, analogous to the way that the [free monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_monoid "Free monoid") provides the underpinning for [formal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language "Formal language").

The power of trace theory stems from the fact that the algebra of [dependency graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependency_graph "Dependency graph") (such as [Petri nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petri_net "Petri net")) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic "Isomorphic") to that of trace monoids, and thus, one can apply both algebraic [formal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language "Formal language") tools, as well as tools from [graph theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_theory "Graph theory").

While the trace monoid had been studied by [Pierre Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Cartier_\(mathematician\) "Pierre Cartier (mathematician)") and [Dominique Foata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Foata "Dominique Foata") for its [combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorics "Combinatorics") in the 1960s, trace theory was first formulated by [Antoni Mazurkiewicz](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni%20Mazurkiewicz%20\(informatyk\) "pl:Antoni Mazurkiewicz (informatyk)") in the 1970s, in an attempt to evade some of the problems in the theory of concurrent computation, including the problems of interleaving and non-deterministic choice with regard to refinement in process calculi.
