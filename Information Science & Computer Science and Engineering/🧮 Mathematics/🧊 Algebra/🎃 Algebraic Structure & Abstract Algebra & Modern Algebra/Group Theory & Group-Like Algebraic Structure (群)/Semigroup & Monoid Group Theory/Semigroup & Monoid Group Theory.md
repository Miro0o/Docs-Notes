### Monoid
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid

In [abstract algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra"), a **monoid** is a set equipped with an [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative "Associative") [binary operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_operation "Binary operation") and an [identity element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_element "Identity element"). For example, the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers "Natural numbers") with addition form a monoid, the identity element being 0.

Monoids are [semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup "Semigroup") with identity. Such [algebraic structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_structure "Algebraic structure") occur in several branches of mathematics.

The functions from a set into itself form a monoid with respect to function composition. More generally, in [category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_theory "Category theory"), the morphisms of an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_\(category_theory\) "Object (category theory)") to itself form a monoid, and, conversely, a monoid may be viewed as a category with a single object.

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science") and [computer programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming "Computer programming"), the set of [strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_\(computer_science\) "String (computer science)") built from a given set of [characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_\(computing\) "Character (computing)") is a [free monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_monoid "Free monoid"). [Transition monoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_monoid "Transition monoid") and [syntactic monoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_monoid "Syntactic monoid") are used in describing [finite-state machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-state_machine "Finite-state machine"). [Trace monoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_monoid "Trace monoid") and [history monoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_monoid "History monoid") provide a foundation for [process calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_calculi "Process calculi") and [concurrent computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_computing "Concurrent computing").

In [theoretical computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_science "Theoretical computer science"), the study of monoids is fundamental for [automata theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automata_theory "Automata theory") ([Krohn–Rhodes theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krohn%E2%80%93Rhodes_theory "Krohn–Rhodes theory")), and [formal language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language_theory "Formal language theory") ([star height problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_height_problem "Star height problem")).

See [semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup "Semigroup") for the history of the subject, and some other general properties of monoids.
