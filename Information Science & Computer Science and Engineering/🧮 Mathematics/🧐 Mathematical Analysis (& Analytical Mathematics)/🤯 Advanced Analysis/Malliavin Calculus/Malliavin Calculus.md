## Intro
> 🔗 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malliavin_calculus

In [probability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_theory "Probability theory") and related fields, **Malliavin calculus** is a set of mathematical techniques and ideas that extend the mathematical field of [calculus of variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variations "Calculus of variations") from deterministic functions to [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_process "Stochastic process"). In particular, it allows the computation of [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative "Derivative") of [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variable "Random variable"). Malliavin calculus is also called the **[stochastic calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_calculus "Stochastic calculus") of variations**. P. Malliavin first initiated the calculus on infinite dimensional space. Then, significant contributors such as S. Kusuoka, D. Stroock, [J-M. Bismut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Michel_Bismut "Jean-Michel Bismut"), [Shinzo Watanabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinzo_Watanabe "Shinzo Watanabe"), I. Shigekawa, and so on completed the foundations for the field.

Malliavin calculus is named after [Paul Malliavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Malliavin "Paul Malliavin") whose ideas led to a [proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof "Mathematical proof") that [Hörmander's condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6rmander's_condition "Hörmander's condition") implies the existence and smoothness of a [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_density_function "Probability density function") for the solution of a [stochastic differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_differential_equation "Stochastic differential equation"); [Hörmander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_H%C3%B6rmander "Lars Hörmander")'s original proof was based on the theory of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation"). The calculus has been applied to [stochastic partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_partial_differential_equation "Stochastic partial differential equation") as well.

The calculus allows [integration by parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration_by_parts "Integration by parts") with random variables; this operation is used in [mathematical finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_finance "Mathematical finance") to compute the sensitivities of [financial derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\) "Derivative (finance)"). The calculus has applications in, for example, [stochastic filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_filtering "Stochastic filtering").
